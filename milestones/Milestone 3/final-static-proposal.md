# Milestone  - Ciara Staveley-O'Carroll

## Description
Birds, Birders, and Chicago

***Where are Chicagoans looking for birds, what are they finding, and which parts of the city are being overlooked?***

Birds: Reported bird counts and species diversity
People: Individual contributors, hotspots, and monthly/yearly participation
Neighborhoods: Reporting activity compared with population and socioeconomic characteristics

The bird: 
What are people seeing?	
- 335 species, nearly 23 million reported bird counts, species richness and bird-count maps

The birders:
Who is documenting them?	
- 10,373 accounts, highly unequal contributions, one observer submitting on 364 days in 2024

The city:
Where and when does birdwatching happen?	
- Montrose Point, community-area differences, hotspots, monthly and yearly activity

For this project, I want to explore where Chicagoans are birdwatching, what they're finding, and which parts of the city receive more attention than others.

Using five years of eBird data, I'm looking at more than 218,000 birdwatching checklists documenting nearly 23 million reported bird counts across Chicago. These records tell us about the city's birds, but they also tell us about the people who choose to look for them.

I'm particularly interested in the relationship between birding activity and the birds being documented. Are the neighborhoods with the most birdwatchers also the places where people record the most birds or the greatest variety of species? Are there unexpected hotspots? And how much can one particularly active citizen scientist influence what we think we know about an entire neighborhood?

I also want to examine how birdwatching changes across seasons and years, and whether participation is spreading throughout Chicago or remaining concentrated in familiar places.

Ultimately, I'm interested in what citizen science makes visible about urban nature, but also what it might leave out. More reported birds don't necessarily mean more birds actually live somewhere. Sometimes, they simply mean more people were looking.
